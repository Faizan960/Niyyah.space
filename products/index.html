The NIFA portfolio

Different problems. Purpose-built products.

Four products in four different markets. They do not necessarily share a backend, a business model or a customer base. What they share is a standard: honest, privacy-conscious, purposeful.

Muslim spiritual wellbeing / Android

Niyyah

Active development. Play Store release being finalised.

Problem

Phones distract from prayer; screen-time tools and prayer apps each solve only half of it.

Product

A calm Muslim companion whose core is a prayer lock that restricts chosen apps during prayer windows until the user verifies prayer, with prayer times, Qibla, Quran, Hadith, Azkar and private reflections.

Intended customers
Practising Muslim smartphone users; students and young professionals; people who want one integrated companion. Families and organisations are a possible future.
Differentiating idea
Combines prayer-window protection with spiritual resources instead of doing only one.
Technology
Android app, local-first data, optional Clerk sign-in and Supabase with Row Level Security.
Revenue hypotheses
Proposed: free core, optional premium, voluntary contributions, ethical sponsorship.

Digital reading and studying / Android application

PaperOS

Proprietary Android product in development.

Problem

Reading and studying on a phone can be scattered across tools and distractions. This is the author’s stated goal, not a research finding.

Product

A proprietary Android reading and study application designed to support a more focused digital reading experience. Feature and technology details are not shown until they can be confirmed.

Intended customers
Students who read and study digitally; Android readers; people who want a dedicated reading and learning environment.
Differentiating idea
A dedicated, focused environment for reading and learning.
Technology
Not yet verified: the repository could not be inspected for this site.
Revenue hypotheses
Potential: individual purchase or subscription, institutional licences, paid customisation or support.

Interactive entertainment / arcade game

Wrong Turn

Still in development. Not released.

Problem

Players follow familiar signals automatically; few games test whether they can follow the rule instead.

Product

A fast psychological arcade game built on the idea that the arrow lies. Rules change, patterns shift and mistakes cost more.

Intended customers
Casual mobile and browser gamers; reaction-game enthusiasts; later competitive casual players and brand or platform partners.
Differentiating idea
Rewards rule-reading and adaptation, not only raw speed.
Technology
Repository layout consistent with a Unity project plus a backend folder; engine not named in the README.
Revenue hypotheses
Proposed: free-to-play, cosmetics, optional ads, premium ad-free, daily challenges, platform distribution, branded licensing.

Privacy-conscious software / Android

QuietDude

Early development. Enforcement not implemented.

Problem

Notifications fragment attention, and OS controls are broad.

Product

Rule-based notification management: users define conditions, priorities and actions, and a deterministic engine evaluates them, with honesty about platform limits.

Intended customers
Students; knowledge workers; people wanting finer notification control; teams in future.
Differentiating idea
Explicit, inspectable rules and conflict handling rather than broad switches.
Technology
Flutter and Dart, Riverpod, Drift/SQLite, rule engine, Kotlin MethodChannel bridge, tests.
Revenue hypotheses
Proposed: free personal, premium advanced rules, one-time purchase or subscription, organisational licensing.

Why these are separate

Different markets, different rules.

The four products serve different people with different expectations. A prayer-focused app, a reading and study app, an arcade game and a notification utility do not share a backend, a pricing model or a trust model, and this site does not suggest they do.

What NIFA provides is the parent brand: a design language, engineering principles and a commitment to describing each product honestly.
