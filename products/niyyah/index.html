Niyyah

Protect your focus. Make space for prayer.

Niyyah (formerly SalahLock) is a calm companion for intentional Muslim living. Its core idea is a prayer lock: chosen distracting apps are restricted during prayer windows and released once you confirm you have prayed. Around that sits a set of spiritual tools: prayer times, Qibla, Quran, Hadith, Azkar and private reflections.

The problem

Phones are useful, and they are also loud at the wrong moments.

Who has it. Muslim smartphone users who want their daily prayers to be uninterrupted, especially students and young professionals whose study, work or leisure apps pull at them around prayer times.

Why current tools fall short. General screen-time tools reduce overall usage but know nothing about prayer. Prayer apps tell you when to pray and offer religious content, but do not usually protect the window itself. Neither connects the two.

Consequence. Prayer can be delayed or interrupted by notifications and habitual app opening. The person intends to pray, and the phone gets in the way.

Evidence status. The problem is described from the author’s product thinking. No user research, survey data or usage statistics are published, so treat the scale of the problem as a hypothesis to validate with real users.
Not a promise. Niyyah does not guarantee religious consistency and does not replace personal responsibility. It makes a deliberate choice easier to keep.

The solution

A prayer-centred approach to digital wellbeing.

Niyyah ties app restriction directly to the prayer schedule. The user chooses which apps to restrict per prayer; shortly before a prayer time, those apps are paused behind a full-screen overlay; the user confirms the prayer by voice or typing, and the lock ends. The same app carries the spiritual resources people already look for around prayer, so the user has a reason to open Niyyah, and then leave the phone.

The README states the design goal plainly: the app should return attention rather than capture it. The ideal journey is to open the app, receive guidance, leave, and pray.

Business logic

From prayer settings to release, step by step.

The prayer-protection workflow as documented. Exact enforcement depends on Android permissions and the device; Niyyah does not claim every app can be blocked on every device.

  1. InputConfigure

    User picks which apps to restrict, per prayer, with Jumu’ah overrides.

  2. InputGet prayer times

    GPS-based times with offline cache, or a saved local-masjid schedule.

  3. ProcessEvaluate the window

    The lock becomes active a 5-minute buffer before the configured prayer time.

  4. OutputRestrict

    Opening a restricted app during an active window shows a full-screen lock overlay.

  5. DecisionVerify the prayer

    Confirm by voice (fuzzy matched) or by typing a short confirmation.

  6. OutputRelease

    A successful match for that prayer ends its lock immediately, without waiting for the countdown.

  7. ProcessRecord locally

    Streaks and progress are stored on the device; monthly reflections are generated on-device.

Where it can fail or fall short

  • Permissions or device settings block enforcement. Android restrictions depend on permissions the user must grant and on the device’s configuration.
  • Speech recognition mis-hears. Voice matching is fuzzy to tolerate mis-transcription, and typing is the alternative route.
  • Genuine need. A temporary pause (travel, illness) and a deliberate, rate-limited emergency override exist so the lock never traps the user.
  • No network. Prayer times are cached so they remain available offline.

Who uses vs who pays

Today the user and the audience are the same person, and the app is free to use as documented. There is no paying institution. An institutional buyer (a mosque, school or family plan) is a possible future split between user and payer, and is unvalidated.

Value proposition

A phone that gets out of the way of prayer, plus the spiritual tools you would otherwise open separate apps for. The value is protected attention, not time spent in the app.

Data lifecycle

Core data is local-first and available offline. Signing in is optional and only enables supported cloud features. Local backups are separate from cloud sync. Data is kept isolated when switching accounts.

Not claimed

The README does not claim end-to-end encryption or sync of every data type, so this site does not either. Data deletion, retention and backup limits are not described in the README and should be documented before release.

Privacy, safety and limitations

What this product does not do.

  • Not every app can be blocked on every device; enforcement depends on Android permissions and the device’s configuration.
  • It does not guarantee religious consistency, and it is not a substitute for personal responsibility.
  • No claim is made that every data type is end-to-end encrypted or synchronised.
  • Voice verification is a convenience gate, not proof of prayer; the app trusts the user’s confirmation.
  • The public Google Play release is still being finalised, so it is not yet available as a store download.
