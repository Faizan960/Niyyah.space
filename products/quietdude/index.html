QuietDude

Your notifications. Your rules.

QuietDude explores a more explicit way to manage notification interruptions: users define conditions, priorities and actions, and a deterministic rule engine evaluates them. It is built to be precise about what the operating system allows.

The problem

Notifications interrupt study, work, sleep and personal time.

Who has it. Students, developers, researchers, writers, designers and anyone who needs sustained concentration, plus people who simply want fewer interruptions.

Why current alternatives fall short. Built-in operating-system controls tend to offer broad categories (per app, do-not-disturb schedules). They do not always let you state exactly when, why and with what priority a notification should behave a certain way.

Consequence. Interruptions fragment attention. People either tolerate them or switch everything off and risk missing what matters.

Evidence status. The problem is widely felt but no QuietDude-specific user research is published. Whether finer rules would change behaviour is a hypothesis.
No promise of total control. Android and iOS limit what any third-party app can do to notifications. QuietDude does not promise complete control over them.

The solution

Rules you can read, order and predict.

A rule combines conditions, an action and a priority. The engine evaluates rules deterministically: the same input always produces the same result, and conflicts are resolved by explicit priority logic. Rules are stored on the device, and the UI can show why a result occurred.

The architecture deliberately separates cross-platform logic from Android-specific capability, so the app can say clearly when something is unsupported instead of pretending.

Business logic

Saved, evaluated, requested, executed: four different things.

QuietDude’s core discipline is to keep these four states separate. A rule being saved is not the same as it being evaluated, a platform action being requested, or that action actually happening.

  1. InputCreate a rule

    The user defines a rule in the app.

  2. InputSet conditions

    Choose from conditions the current app supports.

  3. InputChoose an action and priority

    Pick a supported action and conflict preference.

  4. DecisionValidate and store

    Check the rule, then save it locally in SQLite.

  5. ProcessEvaluate

    The deterministic engine evaluates eligible inputs.

  6. ProcessResolve conflicts

    Higher priority wins, with explicit handling of ties.

  7. OutputRequest through the platform adapter

    Only actions the OS and current build permit are requested.

  8. OutputReport the result

    The interface shows the outcome or the limitation.

Where it can fail or fall short

  • Enforcement is not implemented. The current version does not block, silence, hide or rewrite notifications.
  • The OS may refuse. Even a requested action may not be executed if the platform does not permit it.
  • Unsupported conditions. Universal location triggers or geofencing are not claimed, and iOS enforcement is not claimed.
  • No cloud. Cloud sync and user accounts are not claimed.

Why a typed platform boundary

It keeps cross-platform logic separate from Android-specific capability, and makes unsupported behaviour easy to detect and report instead of failing silently.

Privacy and storage

Privacy, safety and limitations

What this product does not do.

  • Real notification enforcement is not implemented. QuietDude does not currently block, silence, hide or rewrite notifications.
  • Not all notification actions are supported, and none are promised across all Android versions.
  • No location-based triggers, geofencing, iOS enforcement, cloud sync or user accounts are claimed.
  • The repository is private. It is not open-source software and the link may require GitHub sign-in and access.
  • Privacy statements are limited to what the code does; no certification is claimed.
