The brand idea

New Ideas, Future Applications.

A parent brand building purposeful products across different industries, needs, and possibilities.

What the name says. NIFA stands for New Ideas, Future Applications. It is a way of looking at technology: start from an idea worth trying, and aim at an application that will still matter later.

What “with intention” means. Each product has one clear job. If a simpler solution works, NIFA uses it. Nothing is built just because it sounds advanced.

What NIFA is not. It is not another name for Niyyah, and it is not only a wellbeing company, a game studio or a reading-app maker. The portfolio is diverse on purpose.

  • Different industries: faith and wellbeing, reading and learning, entertainment, privacy software.
  • Different needs: attention, focus, instinct, interruption.
  • Different possibilities: each product can grow, or stop, on its own evidence.

Why different domains

Start from problems, not categories.

NIFA begins with a specific problem: attention around prayer, focus while reading and studying, instinct versus rules, and control over interruptions. Each product keeps its own audience, architecture, business model and roadmap.

They are connected by shared standards, not by a shared backend or a shared customer base.

Business approach

One portfolio, four separate bets.

How NIFA thinks about audience and revenue across the whole portfolio. The full reasoning is on the About page.

1 · Problem first

Every product starts from a real friction, then picks the smallest technology that solves it.

2 · Own audience, own model

Worshippers, students, gamers and privacy-minded users adopt and pay in very different ways, so each product plans its own revenue.

3 · Validate before monetising

Revenue ideas stay marked as proposals until a product is released, used and understood. No revenue, users or partners are claimed on this site.

4 · Trust is the constraint

Prayer, reading habits and notifications are personal. A monetisation choice that costs trust is treated as too expensive.

Shared engineering principles

The standard behind four very different products.

  • Purpose over complexity. Cut what does not serve the problem.
  • Useful over novel. If a simpler approach works, use it.
  • Privacy-conscious engineering. Local-first data and minimal collection where the product allows.
  • Honest communication. Say what works, what is planned and what is not possible.
  • Separate identities, one standard. Each product keeps its own personality under a consistent parent brand.
  • Accessible and maintainable. Responsive, keyboard-friendly interfaces and readable code.
